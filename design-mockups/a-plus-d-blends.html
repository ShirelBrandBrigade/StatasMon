Three ways to blend Mission Control and Glass Observatory

Same app, same sample session as the six directions. Each blend mixes A and D along a different seam: one keeps D's shapes and takes A's colors, one keeps A's structure and takes D's colors, and one splits the screen so each parent owns a half. All three keep the multi-device sidebar or switcher, an iPhone, iPad and Android phone connected at once, and the full feature list.

  • AD-1Dark Glass
  • AD-2Light Instrument
  • AD-3Two-tone Workbench
AD-1

Dark Glass

D's layout and components on A's dark palette.

Takes from A: the dark ground, cyan/violet/amber data colors, monospace figures, the recording status, and per-core bars and stacked breakdowns inside every card. Takes from D: the device sidebar, the plain-language headline, ring gauges, rounded frosted cards and the plain log feed.

Tradeoff. The friendliest of the three at a glance and the closest to D's flow, with A's density folded into each card. The frosted cards need a real GPU-composited window to look as intended.

AD-2

Light Instrument

A's structure and density on D's light palette.

Takes from A: the icon rail, the fixed nine-panel grid where every metric is always visible, the device switcher and the dense log with level toggles. Takes from D: the light ground with a faint tint, rounded white cards, soft blue/violet/mint/peach accents and a one-line status sentence with pills in the top bar.

Tradeoff. The most information per pixel of the three and the easiest to read in a bright room, but the least 'calm': nothing is summarized, everything competes equally.

AD-3

Two-tone Workbench

A's dark sidebar beside D's light content, with a summary tier above a detail tier.

Takes from A: the dark device rail with recording controls, the compact detail grid with core bars and dense tables, and the log with levels. Takes from D: the headline sentence, a single summary strip of small rings, white cards on a tinted ground. Adds one UX idea from neither: a Focus chip that pins one app so processes and the log highlight it, with per-app export.

Tradeoff. The most opinionated hierarchy (summary, then details, then evidence), so it scales best as features grow; but two palettes on one screen must be tuned carefully or the seam shows.
